How the backend works

Everything comes in, gets read and matched, and reaches you once, with its evidence.

1 · In

Every channel

  • Your EMR: notes, orders, results, letters, medicines, bookings
  • Lab portals and faxes
  • Hospital and specialist letters
  • eHealth records
  • Patients' WhatsApp
2 · Read

Agents

  • Match each document to the right chart
  • Quote what it says, word for word
  • Check what's already been done
  • Answer patients only from your approved replies
  • Fixed rules for red flags
3 · Queue

One place

  • Most urgent first, with a due time
  • Routed to whoever is working
  • Paged if critical and unseen for an hour
  • Raised if left untouched
4 · You

Decide

  • Sign, edit or see in person
  • Prescribe
  • Close the item
5 · Watch

Until it's done

  • Messages, bookings and dispensing slips go out
  • Chased until the result, letter or visit is back
  • Back in your queue if it isn't

Agents can open an item, raise its urgency, alert and page you. They can't close, dismiss or downgrade anything, and they never prescribe. Switching the backend off should leave your clinic less safe, never more.

Tested before it touches a patient

The engine has run this clinic's whole year.

LoopBench simulates a solo Hong Kong GP on Apricot VITA for a year, with real lab report formats, fax, courier and portal delays, a locum, a Lunar New Year closure and a hidden answer key. Every item the engine opens is checked like a code review: a quote that isn't word for word in its record is a hard fail.

What it still misses is listed openly: 2 of 4 feed outages were flagged on time (the lab portal and the courier), while the fax and eHealth outages were missed. Mr Chan's result landed 10 minutes after the 19:30 run and waited until 08:30 for the next one. The WhatsApp side isn't in the simulator yet.

The rules

What the agents may do, and what stays yours

Agents mayRead and match documents, quote them, check the chart, ask patients questions and send reminders from your approved replies, book within your rules, open items, raise urgency, alert and page you.
Only youPrescribe, change a dose, tell a patient what a result means, sign anything clinical, and close, dismiss or downgrade an item.
Fixed, never a modelRed-flag replies (call 999), and what happens when a critical result is unseen.
Remote repeatsOnly for patients you've seen in person in the last 6 months, on an unchanged dose, with no open item on their chart. Never dangerous drugs, sedatives or antibiotics.

Remote repeats follow the Medical Council of Hong Kong's Ethical Guidelines on Practice of Telemedicine (December 2019): identify the patient, an adequate evaluation, instructions understood, the doctor responsible, records kept. A starting map, not legal advice.

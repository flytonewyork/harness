The rules

How a remote refill meets the Medical Council's telemedicine duties

Identify the patientThe WhatsApp number is linked to the chart at an in-person visit. Name and date of birth are checked on the first message.
An adequate evaluationOnly patients you've seen in person in the last 6 months, on an unchanged dose. The care team collects readings, side effects and red-flag answers, and you see them before you sign.
Instructions understoodThe plan goes in writing, in Cantonese or English: what to take, when, and when to come back.
The doctor stays responsibleNothing is prescribed until you sign. The care team can ask, hold, alert and page. It never prescribes, and never closes a loop.
RecordsEvery message, check and decision is kept with its evidence, ready for your clinic system.

From the Medical Council of Hong Kong's Ethical Guidelines on Practice of Telemedicine (December 2019). A starting map, not legal advice.

Never through the refill desk

  • Dangerous drugs, sedatives and antibiotics.
  • New medicines or dose changes. Those are a consultation.
  • Anyone with an open abnormal result, overdue monitoring or a red flag. They come to you first, as Mr Chan and Mrs Lau did.
